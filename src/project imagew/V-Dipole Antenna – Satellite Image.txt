V-Dipole Antenna – Satellite Image Reception System
Project Overview

The V-Dipole Antenna is a student-designed ground-station antenna developed by Antariksh Club, Dhole Patil College of Engineering, Pune, for receiving radio transmissions from Low Earth Orbit (LEO) weather satellites.

The antenna was specifically designed and tuned around the 137 MHz VHF frequency range, enabling reception of real-time weather imagery transmitted by satellites such as METEOR-M2-3.

The system provides a practical orbit-to-ground communication link, allowing students to directly receive satellite signals and process them into usable Earth-observation imagery.

The system operates through three primary stages:

1. Satellite Signal Transmission

The METEOR-M2-3 weather satellite transmits meteorological Earth-observation data using a radio downlink in the 137 MHz VHF band.

2. Ground Signal Reception

A student-designed 137 MHz V-Dipole antenna, installed on the rooftop of Dhole Patil College of Engineering, receives the satellite's radio signal during its visible pass over the ground station.

The V-Dipole provides a simple and practical antenna configuration suitable for VHF satellite reception.

3. SDR Reception & Image Decoding

The received RF signal is captured using an SDR (Software Defined Radio).

The signal was recorded at approximately 137.900 MHz and subsequently processed using SatDump, which demodulates and decodes the received satellite transmission into weather imagery.

Major Components
1. V-Dipole Antenna
Student-designed and constructed antenna.
Tuned for approximately 137 MHz.
Installed on the rooftop of Dhole Patil College of Engineering, Pune.
Used as the primary RF receiving element.
Designed for receiving VHF transmissions from passing weather satellites.
2. Software Defined Radio (SDR)

The SDR acts as the RF receiver between the antenna and the computer.

It:

Receives the VHF signal from the antenna.
Tunes to the satellite transmission frequency.
Digitizes the received RF signal.
Allows the signal to be recorded for further processing.
Provides a software-controlled alternative to conventional dedicated radio receivers.
3. SatDump

SatDump is used for processing and decoding the captured satellite signal.

The processing chain is essentially:

RF Signal → SDR → Digital Signal → SatDump → Decoded Image

Operational Ground Station

The presentation also states that this V-Dipole system became the second operational ground station of Antariksh Club, alongside the club's existing UHF station.

The club was also working toward developing a geostationary satellite receiving station.

This demonstrates a progression from receiving different types of satellite transmissions toward developing a more capable student-operated ground-station infrastructure.
