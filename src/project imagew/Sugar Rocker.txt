# Rocket Propulsion Testing & Experimental Analysis

**Project Type:** Rocketry Research and Testing
**Club:** Antariksh Club
**College:** Dhole Patil College of Engineering, Pune
**Proposed Budget:** ₹1,000/-

## About the Project

The Antariksh Club is working towards its long-term objective of designing and developing a student-built sounding rocket within the next two years.

As an initial step towards this objective, the club has conducted preliminary experiments to understand basic rocket propulsion, ignition behaviour, motor casing behaviour, and combustion characteristics.

During the initial experimental campaign, the team conducted four test runs, out of which two produced partially successful results. These experiments provided useful practical observations and highlighted the need for a more systematic and quantitative testing methodology.

The project focuses on moving from preliminary trial-based experimentation towards properly documented propulsion testing, thrust measurement, and experimental data analysis.

## Project Objectives

* Study the basic performance characteristics of the experimental propulsion system.
* Develop a systematic method for recording experimental observations.
* Measure and analyse thrust during controlled ground testing.
* Record test parameters such as mass, test duration, ignition response, and post-test condition.
* Study the relationship between experimental observations and theoretical expectations.
* Identify sources of inconsistency between different test runs.
* Develop basic instrumentation and testing practices required for future rocketry projects.
* Establish a foundation for the club’s long-term sounding-rocket development program.

## Previous Experimental Work

The Antariksh Club has already conducted four preliminary experimental test runs.

**2 Partially Successful Tests:** Observable combustion and thrust were obtained, but the desired overall performance was not achieved.

**2 Unsuccessful / Inconclusive Tests:** Primarily due to difficulties associated with experimental consistency and ignition/testing conditions.

During the testing, the team observed variations in ignition behaviour and combustion performance. The experiments demonstrated that visual observation alone is insufficient for determining propulsion performance.

The previous static-test report also identified inconsistencies in the propellant grain, including small internal air pockets, which were associated with uneven and inefficient burning.

## Proposed Testing Approach

The next phase will focus on measurement and analysis rather than repeated trial-and-error experimentation.

The team proposes to develop a basic experimental test setup capable of recording thrust and relevant test parameters.

The testing program will include:

1. Controlled ground testing under faculty supervision.
2. Measurement of thrust using a suitable force-measurement arrangement.
3. Recording of motor/test-article mass before and after testing.
4. Recording of ignition and burn-duration observations.
5. Documentation of each test using a standardized test sheet.
6. Comparison of results between different test runs.
7. Analysis of thrust measurements and identification of performance inconsistencies.
8. Preparation of a test report containing experimental data, observations, conclusions, and recommendations.

## Expected Outcomes

At the completion of this testing phase, the team expects to obtain:

* A basic thrust-testing capability.
* Quantitative thrust measurements instead of visual estimation alone.
* Standardized experimental test records.
* Comparative data from multiple test runs.
* Better understanding of experimental uncertainties and inconsistencies.
* Initial propulsion-performance data to support future engineering analysis.
* A foundation for developing the avionics, telemetry, structural, aerodynamic, and propulsion subsystems required for the club’s future sounding-rocket project.

## Long-Term Vision

The proposed activity is not intended merely as a repeated trial of an experimental rocket motor. It is the beginning of a structured Rocketry Research and Testing Program within the Antariksh Club.

The long-term development pathway is planned as:

**Research → Design → Simulation → Ground Testing → Data Analysis → Validation → Controlled Flight Testing → Sounding Rocket Development**

The knowledge and experimental infrastructure developed during this phase will subsequently support work on rocket avionics, telemetry, aerodynamic stability, recovery systems, structural design, and flight-data acquisition.

## Project Outcome

The four preliminary tests have provided the Antariksh Club with valuable practical experience and have demonstrated the need for a more scientific and quantitative approach to rocketry experimentation.

The proposed testing program will allow the team to move from basic trial-and-error experimentation towards measurement-based engineering, with particular emphasis on thrust measurement, repeatability, documentation, and data analysis.

Approval of the proposed ₹1,000 budget will enable the club to establish the basic testing resources required for the next stage of its long-term goal of developing a student sounding rocket.
