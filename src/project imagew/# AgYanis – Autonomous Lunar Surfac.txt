## About the Project

The AgYanis team from the Antariksh Club developed an Autonomous Lunar Surface 3D-Mapping Rover for exploring and analysing a simulated lunar surface containing craters, uneven terrain, and obstacles.

The project was developed as part of the National Space Innovation Challenge (NSIC) 2026 and provided students with practical experience in space exploration, robotics, autonomous systems, computer vision, and planetary terrain analysis.

The rover integrates stereo vision, LiDAR, autonomous navigation, and hazard detection to analyse its surroundings and navigate through simulated lunar terrain.

## Key Features

The AgYanis rover uses:

* Raspberry Pi 5 as the main computing platform.
* Two Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 cameras for stereo vision.
* TF-Luna 1D LiDAR for distance calibration and safety detection.
* Encoder-based four-wheel-drive system for movement and odometry.
* DHT11 sensor for temperature and humidity monitoring.
* LED lighting for darker portions of the simulated terrain.
* Python and C++ for control, vision, and mapping software.

## Stereo-Based 3D Mapping

The rover uses two cameras positioned to capture the same terrain from slightly different viewpoints.

The difference between the two images is processed to estimate depth. The generated depth information is then converted into a 3D representation of the terrain.

This allows the rover to analyse surface variations and identify possible hazards.

The TF-Luna LiDAR provides an independent distance measurement that can be used for calibration and additional safety monitoring.

## Operating Modes

### Manual Mode

In Manual Mode, the operator controls the rover while the system continuously provides sensor information and hazard warnings through the dashboard.

### Autonomous Mode

In Autonomous Mode, the rover analyses the generated depth information, identifies hazardous regions, and attempts to navigate through safer portions of the simulated lunar terrain.

The combination of stereo vision, LiDAR, autonomous navigation, and real-time monitoring demonstrates the integration of multiple engineering disciplines into a single working prototype.

## Interdisciplinary Collaboration

The physical development of the AgYanis rover involved collaboration with the **DPCOE Graphics Design Club**.

The Graphics Design Club provided technical assistance in **3D printing the complete rover chassis**, helping the hardware team manufacture and assemble the physical rover prototype within the available development period.

This collaboration demonstrated how expertise from different student clubs can contribute to an interdisciplinary engineering project.

## Expert Feedback

During the NSIC 2026 evaluation process, the team received valuable guidance from a former ISRO scientist.

The expert appreciated the interdisciplinary approach of the project and encouraged the students to develop the work further as research.

The stereo-vision approach was identified as an interesting area for further study because two viewpoints are used to estimate depth, similar to how information from different viewpoints contributes to depth perception.

The rover’s hazard-response system was also identified as an area with potential for further research.

## Future Scope

The AgYanis rover can be further improved through:

* Better stereo calibration.
* LiDAR-camera fusion.
* Terrain classification.
* Improved hazard detection.
* Autonomous path planning.
* More accurate 3D mapping.

The Antariksh Club also plans to pursue research based on the project, with the proposed topic:

**“Stereo-based 3D planetary perception and hazard-aware autonomous navigation.”**
